Sample report · The decision layer

What to reorder, and what not to promote again

The weekly report shows what happened. This is the part that answers what to do about it: how much to order for each store next week, and which promotions last month quietly lost money while the volume numbers looked fine.

Both come from models rather than rules of thumb. Reorder quantities are scored in an inventory simulation, and a promotion only counts as incremental if it would have sold anyway.

Quantities are safety stock and reorder points driven by a demand forecast — z × σ × √(protection period) — then scored in a period-by-period simulation with lead time, review period and backorders. A fixed days-of-supply rule gives 81% fill rate on the same demand.

Promotions that did not pay back

  • “Buy 2 get 1” on Rendang, week 36 — volume up 22%, margin down Rp 6.4jt Elasticity of −1.9 against a true −1.2. The extra units were going to sell anyway.
  • Flash sale on Nasi Kuning, week 37 — 41% of units sold at a loss Discount applied to every unit, including the ones that were not incremental.
  • Bundle discount on the sambal range, week 37 — cannibalised Sambal Ijo Shifted demand between two products on the same shelf rather than adding any.

Promoted units only count as incremental when they would not have sold otherwise. The baseline here is trained on your own non-promoted store-weeks, so the comparison is like-for-like rather than against last month’s total.
