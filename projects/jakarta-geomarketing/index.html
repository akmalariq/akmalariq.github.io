Jakarta Retail Site Selection

Where in DKI Jakarta is the strongest opportunity for a new F&B or retail outlet? This platform scores every H3 hexagon in the city using competitor density and the mix of demand-generating places around it, built from 289,000 OpenStreetMap-derived points of interest published by Overture Maps, modelled in BigQuery with dbt, and rendered as the interactive map below.

How the score works

opportunity = w_demand × demand_score + w_diversity × diversity_score + w_low_competition × (100 − competition_score) weights (dbt variables): demand 0.55 | diversity 0.25 | low competition 0.20
  • demand_score: number of demand-generating places in the cell (offices, education, retail anchors, lodging, lifestyle, health, leisure), min-max normalised across all cells.
  • diversity_score: how many different demand categories are present, so a mixed-use cell beats a single-purpose one.
  • competition_score: density of F&B competitors (coffee shops, cafes, restaurants, bakeries, bars and similar). It enters the score reversed: high competition lowers the opportunity.
  • Every component is kept in the mart, so any cell can be explained by its raw counts rather than a single opaque number.

Pipeline

Overture Maps places (GeoParquet, DKI Jakarta bbox) → Cloud Storage (raw + prepared) → BigQuery (raw_places, raw_h3_cells) → dbt: staging → intermediate → marts → JSON export → this map
  • 289,015 places downloaded, 287k of them inside the candidate cells after filtering.
  • H3 cells are assigned with the reference h3 library, then all business logic runs in dbt on BigQuery: classification, aggregation, normalisation, scoring.
  • The opportunity formula, including its weights, is a dbt variable, and two data tests enforce that the weights sum to 1 and that every score stays inside 0 to 100.
  • Everything runs inside the always-free tier of Google Cloud, except a single small Dataflow batch job used for the ingestion step.

Honest caveats

POI coverage is not uniform. Overture places derive from OpenStreetMap, and mapping activity varies between neighbourhoods. A cell can score lower because it is genuinely quieter, or simply because it is less mapped. Treat the map as a prioritisation tool, not a census.

This is a heuristic, not a validated model. There is no sales, footfall, rent, or lease data here, so the score has not been calibrated against real revenue. The weights are deliberate judgement calls, exposed as dbt variables so they can be challenged and changed.

Scores are relative to DKI Jakarta. Normalisation is min-max within this dataset, so a score of 90 means "high for Jakarta", not "high globally". Comparing cities would need a shared baseline.

What the model ignores: opening hours, brands, price positioning, floor area, rent, competition quality, and physical barriers such as rivers and toll roads. Distance calculations are straight-line, not travel time.
